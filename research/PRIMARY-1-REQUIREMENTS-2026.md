# Primary 1 mathematics: requirements and completion plan

**Subsequent user decision, 20 October 2026:** external resources are unavailable;
use best approximation and document a whole-year plan. The active
[Primary 1 production plan](../docs/PRIMARY-1-PRODUCTION-PLAN.md) and
[assumption register](PRIMARY-1-ASSUMPTIONS.json) now supply the working curriculum,
calendar and textbook/workbook defaults. Do not treat the acquisition priority
below as a blocker to provisional drafting. Source authenticity, current alignment,
local adoption and actual testing remain unverified; no research claim is upgraded
by the user's permission to approximate.

Research date: **20 October 2026**. Intended users: **both public and private
schools**, confirmed by the user. Product direction: separate Primary 1, Primary 2
and Primary 3 books. This report concerns Primary 1 mathematics, not all Primary 1
subjects. It separates verified historical requirements, current official policy,
provisional edition evidence and editorial recommendations.

## Decision supported by the evidence

We have a useful manuscript foundation, not a finished Primary 1 book. Retain the
19 existing units and stable IDs as the starting inventory, but expand compressed
teaching sequences and bring practical skills into pupil-facing activities.

**Recommended product:** a reusable Primary 1 pupil textbook plus a separately
identified write-in workbook. Preserve the stories, exact diagrams and S1-S9
teaching sequence across the pair. Confirm the original Gombe circular and its
scope before locking this format; the current combined write-in book should not
be assumed suitable for school adoption merely because its mathematics is sound.

Two decisions precede any claim of completion: acquire the current official
Primary 1 Mathematics curriculum/teacher guide, and confirm Gombe's applicable
scheme and textbook/workbook directive. Useful drafting can continue against the
historical baseline, explicitly labelled as such.

## Historical subject scope and what needs completing

The following is a planning synthesis of the **existing local 2012 register and
manuscript audit**, not a new national syllabus. See the [54-row matrix](PRIMARY-1-GAP-MATRIX.csv)
for individual objective IDs, bounds, lesson/question links and actions.

| Area | Historical baseline | Existing material | Work required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early number | Sort, count, recognise, write and order 1-5; understand/read/write zero; extend these skills through nine | P1-01 to P1-04 | Finish Chapter 1 migration/review. Keep Chapter 2's sorting/comparison story. Provide independent physical sorting plus count/read/write checks; strengthen 6/9 formation and recognition. |
| Ten and larger numbers | Ten as a group; tens/ones with activities through twenty; recognise/read/write through 99 | P1-05, P1-06 | Expand into manageable sequences: ten, teens, twenty, later decades and transitions. Six questions cannot establish understanding of the entire 1-99 range. |
| Fractions | Recognise halves and quarters using objects and shapes | P1-07 | Teach halves before quarters, equal versus unequal parts, varied wholes and independent recognition as well as drawing. |
| Addition | Small totals below 5 and below 10; two/three addends through 18; two-digit addition without regrouping with total at most 40; checking | P1-08 to P1-10 | Model three-addend totals above nine before assessment. Separate concrete joining, symbols/equality, count-on, adding zero, accuracy checks and tens/ones progression. |
| Subtraction | Subtract within 9 and through 18; check results | P1-11 | A single 8-minus-3 example is insufficient support for 18-minus-9. Model the larger range and crossing ten, then provide guided and fresh checks. |
| Missing numbers and accuracy | Missing values in addition/subtraction and related open statements; everyday correctness checks | P1-12 | Expand hidden-part tasks, different blank positions, equality and contextual error correction; keep all six source objective IDs even when experiences overlap. |
| Money | Genuine coin recognition and tracing; historical token sums through 25k and change from payments through 10k | P1-13 | Obtain verified recognition resources and a real coin for tracing. Keep historical pretend arithmetic clearly distinct from current currency recognition and everyday prices. Confirm applicable revised scope before final authoring. |
| Length and distance | Compare/order actual lengths; measure with non-standard natural units; apply locally | P1-14 | Add physical ordering and genuine path-distance measurement. Ordering printed numbers or measuring only a book does not demonstrate every practical skill. |
| Time | Everyday sequence and timing of home and school activities | P1-15 | Include different parts of the day and both home/school examples. Historical baseline does not require clock reading. Current heading ambiguity remains open. |
| Weight | Qualitative light/heavy comparisons using actual objects | P1-16 | Show balance results clearly, distinguish prediction from observation, and include a fresh practical comparison. |
| Solids | Sort/name cube, cuboid, cylinder and sphere; compare cube/non-cube cuboid; familiar examples | P1-17 | Sort all four solids, not just rolling a ball versus a box. Include handling and familiar examples. Preserve the fact that a cube is a special cuboid. |
| Flat shapes | Recognise/match/name square, rectangle and circle; distinguish examples; household connections | P1-18 | Add explicit picture/name matching and rotated examples. Pattern continuation and property questions do not replace recognition. Preserve square-as-special-rectangle accuracy. |
| Data | Collect ages and heights | P1-19 | Model asking, observing/measuring consistently and recording. Reading supplied data is preparation, not evidence of collecting data. Provide voluntary/private or fictional-role alternatives. |

The historical addition source disagrees between total below 18 in one column and
at most 18 in another. The manuscript follows the performance objective's inclusive
bound; retain the discrepancy for educator review. Do not increase number ranges
or import multiplication, division, capacity, advanced graphs or measurement
calculations from later classes without explicit evidence or an enrichment label.

## Recommended book/workbook arrangement

The reusable textbook would contain the child and teacher introductions, learning
goals, stories, exact teaching diagrams, worked examples, shared/practical tasks,
oral recall and exercise prompts answered in an exercise book or workbook. It
should not depend on pupils marking the textbook for an activity to work.

The separate workbook would contain tracing, matching lines, circling, drawing,
written answers, practice records and assessment response spaces. Link every
workbook task to its textbook lesson with stable adult IDs and clear pupil-facing
navigation. Paper-and-object activities must work without a phone or internet.

Keep class-specific teacher guidance, answers, misconceptions, interventions,
fresh/delayed checks, glossary, curriculum evidence, credits and next-class preview
within the class textbook under the existing project standard. That standard also
requires the final four pupil teaching pages to introduce AI offline. These are
**existing project requirements**, not national P1 mathematics mandates established
by this research. The new three-book/workbook architecture needs an explicit
project decision and reconciliation of the standards before changing support
placement, allocating the AI pages across books, or omitting them. No such change
is implemented or approved here.

Current Chapter 2 directly supplies reusable story/model material and workbook
task material. Separating those activities and changing navigation/layout will
require a new rendered-page review; its prior approval cannot transfer automatically.
Chapter 1 introductions can become shared front matter, with its counting lesson
retained in the pupil sequence rather than repeating introductions per chapter.

## Proposed teaching progression

These are editorial blocks, **not an official term scheme** or fixed chapter count.

1. Starting check; 1-5; sorting/matching/order; zero; 6-9; a group of ten.
2. Teens/twenty, later decades and reading/writing through 99, paced in small steps.
   Begin simple joining/taking-away experiences once small quantities are secure;
   these need not wait until every number through 99 is learned.
3. Gradual addition and subtraction, missing parts, everyday checking; halves and
   quarters after equal sharing of small collections/wholes is secure.
4. Money, comparing/measuring length and distance, routines/time, qualitative weight,
   solids/flat shapes and collection of simple data, with earlier number practice
   revisited throughout.

Use three revision checkpoints and a final assessment framework as an editorial
starting point, adapted to the confirmed school calendar/scheme. Do not rename
every small teaching step as a long chapter or multiply Chapter 2's seven pupil
pages by nineteen to manufacture a page budget.
