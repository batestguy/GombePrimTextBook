# Primary textbook design: evidence and recommendations

Current chapter authority: [CHAPTER-STANDARD.md](../docs/CHAPTER-STANDARD.md) and [independent review](../docs/CHAPTER-REVIEW.md). Historical research/review below is evidence, not approval of subsequent chapters.

Research date: 18 October 2026. Scope: primary mathematics, especially ages 5-9,
for the Gombe Primary 1-3 write-in volume. This is a focused design review, not
a representative survey of Nigerian schools, a product ranking, or a classroom trial.
No lesson or artwork was changed during this research.

## Main finding

Strong published resources already use practical models, discussion, reasoning,
characters and assessment. Our opportunity is to combine these instructional
strengths with coherent local stories, accessible participation and an offline,
single-volume design that teachers can use with ordinary materials.

Current availability does not mean a newly authored edition. The inspected
Hachette sample is a 2021 edition; White Rose's sample is revised 2022; Learn
Africa's guide identifies its fourth edition as 2019, despite its 2026 upload path.
Publication dates were taken from the documents, not search-engine dates.

## Evidence and implications

**Pictures need a teaching purpose.** Eng, Godwin and Fisher's 2020 experiment
found better comprehension with streamlined illustrations in beginning readers;
it studied first/second-grade reading, not this mathematics book. It supports
testing removal of irrelevant detail, not removing all illustration or claiming
that sparse pages always teach better. Keep rich story scenes at openings and
clean, exact diagrams beside assessed tasks.
[Primary research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-020-00073-5).
The full article fetch failed; the publisher's detailed abstract and university
research summary were accessible. [Research-team university summary](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2020/september/busy-pictures-hinder-reading.html).

**Objects and diagrams need explicit connections.** Cambridge describes movement
between objects, pictures and mathematical symbols, with the links taught rather
than assumed. A picture of counters beside a numeral is an opportunity to explain
that relationship; it does not establish that a pupil has understood it.
[Cambridge teaching guidance](https://cambridge-community.org.uk/guide-to/cambridge-primary/mathematics/).

**Story and play can support mathematics.** EEF's early mathematics guidance covers
ages three to seven and supports practical experiences, mathematical talk and
well-chosen storybooks. For us, a child's story problem must cause a meaningful
counting, comparison or checking action. A place name alone adds little mathematical
purpose. Evidence needs local classroom testing before claims about our book.
[EEF guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/early-maths/).

**Check and revisit learning.** IES guidance supports spacing learning, connecting
representations, alternating examples and practice, and explanatory questions.
Its early mathematics guide recommends monitoring what children know; evidence
ratings vary, and the early guide specifically targets preschool/kindergarten.
These are grounds for a small oral check and later retrieval opportunities, not
proof that an exact proposed lesson template is optimal for Gombe Primary 1.
[IES learning guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1),
[IES early mathematics guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/18).

**Representation and access are separate obligations.** UNICEF's work includes
narration, image descriptions, alternative formats and offline digital access.
An illustrated wheelchair user is valuable representation but does not make a
PDF usable with a screen reader. Our prototype's embedded fonts and unclipped
text do not establish tagged reading order or accessible mathematical content.
[UNICEF emerging lessons](https://www.unicef.org/accessibletextbooksforall/media/1066/file/UNICEF%20ADT%20Emerging%20Lessons.pdf).

**Audit roles as well as appearances.** UNESCO's 2024 guidance, updated in 2026,
addresses prejudice and stereotyping in educational materials. Rotate who asks,
explains, invents, makes mistakes and helps. Keep Aishatu's requested hijab;
do not derive anyone's ethnicity, ability or personality from clothing or a name.
[UNESCO guidance](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/unmasking-racism-guidelines-educational-materials).

## What our sample should improve

These are editorial judgments from the seven-page v2 sample, not pupil-test results.

| Issue in our sample | Proposed correction | Observable review criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Introductions are warm but contain long paragraphs and adult phrasing such as 'family has roots' | Keep the backstories first; split them into short speech turns and concrete sentences, with fuller shared-reading material retained | A teacher can read each aloud naturally; pupils can identify one interest per friend |
| Counting models show only straight rows | Add a five-frame and a scattered set; explicitly show that rearranging a set does not change how many | A pupil counts the same quantity in a new arrangement |
| Numeral writing is largely copying a complete model | Add a model, supported formation and independent numeral recognition/writing as separate tasks | Recognition of a shuffled numeral is checked separately from handwriting |
| Main practice includes counts of three and five but limited independent sampling of the whole range | Add short opportunities to make or identify sets of one, two and four without changing existing question IDs | Every target quantity is sampled across practical and written opportunities |
| Q6 asks for a better method without showing the mistake | Add a deterministic two-stage example of a counted counter being counted again, then let the pupil demonstrate a repair | Pupil identifies what was counted twice and touches/moves each object once |
| Teacher notes offer general advice but no compact response guide | Add 'If the child does this / try this / recheck' for skipped objects, double counting and recounting without stating the total | Teacher has a specific next action after an observed response |
| 'I can' statement is self-report | Add a brief adult-observed task using a fresh set, and revisit it in later lessons | Completion of a page is not reported as mastery |
| Vocabulary is mostly in teacher notes | Put count / number / how many into small, relevant pupil-facing examples | Vocabulary can be encountered during the action it describes |
| Seven-page sample could be mistaken for the template for every lesson | Use character introductions once at the front; vary lesson length according to need | Full-book page budget preserves writing room without repeating biographies |
| Decorative RGB page fill is digitally attractive but physically untested | Test actual-size print, pencil visibility, gutter clearance, show-through and grayscale legibility | Real findings recorded by a teacher/printer; no assumed approval |

## Proposed teaching rhythm

1. **Meet a problem:** a short character situation with a mathematical reason to act.
2. **Try with objects:** use large safe counters or equivalent available materials.
3. **See the connection:** exact picture linked explicitly to spoken words/numeral.
4. **Try together:** one example with a visible strategy and a useful question.
5. **Practise independently:** varied tasks with diagrams kept beside questions.
6. **Explain or repair:** reason about a character's recoverable mistake.
7. **Show what you know:** a short fresh task, with concrete teacher next steps.
8. **Meet it again:** revisit through a later story or activity.

This is a proposed editorial pattern, not an official scheme or a rigid page count.
Do not force every chapter into every activity format if the concept does not fit.

## Recommended next revision and validation

First strengthen the same counting sample: shorter spoken introductions; clearer
objects-to-picture-to-number explanation; varied arrangements; one visible
misconception; a brief diagnostic and teacher response guide. Preserve the local
characters, hijab, safe materials, exact diagrams and generous writing space.

Then ask a Gombe educator and a small group of appropriately consented pupils to
try it. Observe misunderstood instructions, pointing/double counting, independent
responses, requests for help and physical writing room. Check it again after a
delay. A small usability trial guides revision; it cannot establish effectiveness
or justify general learning-gain claims. Keep required curriculum checking separate:
the [NERDC revised curriculum index](https://www.nerdc.gov.ng/content_manager/new_curriculum_home.html)
does not itself establish a match between all current objectives and our lessons.

No new classroom trial, current NERDC alignment, physical print test or accessibility
certification is claimed by this research.

## Implementation checkpoint

The user subsequently authorized a short chapter applying these recommendations.
The resulting [v3 sample](../output/pdf/P1-01-One-to-five-review-v3.pdf) keeps the
backstories and hijab, shortens spoken introductions and adds visible counting
stages, varied arrangements, a demonstrated misconception, fresh assessment,
teacher response guidance and a later check. See
[artifact review](../output/prototypes/p1-01-v3/REVIEW.md) for validation and remaining
limits. Its learning effectiveness has not been tested with pupils.
