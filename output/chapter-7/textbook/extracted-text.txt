Complete answers and observation
Adult reference / Read with both exact chapter books.
Chapter seven / Educational review edition
Source questions (textbook p7; workbook pp2-3). Q1: 2 equal parts. Q2: 4 equal
parts. Q3: rectangle divided into two equal-area parts; middle vertical/horizontal or
corner-to-corner diagonal accepted. Q4: square with four equal-area parts, exactly one
shaded; grid or four centre-meeting triangles accepted. Q5: No; unequal amounts are
not halves. Q6: whole has four equal parts and exactly one is shaded. Accept equivalent
spoken wording.
Models and guided turns (textbook pp2-6; workbook p1). HALF: two matching
equal parts, unchanged when turned. QUARTER: four matching parts after
perpendicular folds, one shaded. COMPARE: left .3/.7 unequal, right .5/.5 halves.
DIAGONAL: two equal triangles each half. ROTATED: four equal triangles, one shaded
quarter. FRUIT: two equal parts, one shaded half. NOTATION: 1/2 one half; 1/4 one
quarter. DRAW: rectangle middle line; square middle cross with one part shaded.
Workbook p1: top-left half, top-right quarter; bottom-left no, bottom-right yes. Require
amount as well as count.
Support, recheck and extension (textbook p8; workbook p4). Helped rectangle:
two equal halves. New tall rectangle: one middle line makes equal halves; new square:
four equal parts, one indicated quarter. A different half may be horizontal, vertical or
diagonal. Test equal area by folding/overlay, not matching shape alone;
different-shaped pieces can also have equal area. Adults may verify other
mathematically valid partitions.

Story (textbook p9; workbook p5). One square whole, four equal quarters, one

Fresh tasks (textbook p10; workbook p6). Left: half, two equal parts, one
shaded despite rotation. Middle paper fruit: quarter, four equal parts, one shaded.
Right: neither, two unequal parts (.3/.7); shading alone does not name a fraction.
New drawing: four equal-area parts of a whole with exactly one shaded. Recall: half
one of two equal parts; quarter one of four equal parts.
Intervene, then new recheck. If counting alone: overlap unequal paper pieces to
show unequal space. Match folded edges before naming halves. If all shaded parts
called quarters: compare equal two-part and equal four-part wholes, touching each
part. If rotation confuses: turn the same sheet slowly. After help, give a NEW wide
rectangle for halves and NEW square for quarters. Do not score coached work as
independent.
Delayed recall next session. With blank paper and no models, ask the pupil to make
equal halves of a new rectangle and quarters of a new square, point to one part of each
and explain. Present a square split diagonally and a paper orange split into unequal
pieces: half; not halves. Check again in later weeks with different orientations. Record
assistance.

Words, evidence and next steps
Adult reference / Read with both exact chapter books.
Chapter seven / Educational review edition
Glossary. Whole: the complete object or shape being shared. Equal parts: parts
with the same amount of area (space inside the shape). Half: one of two equal parts
of a whole. Quarter: one of four equal parts. Fold: bend paper so parts lie on each
other. Unequal: different amounts. Symbols 1/2 and 1/4 are read only after concrete
models; symbol writing is not required here.
Curriculum mapping. Historical baseline P1-p03-03: recognise halves and quarters of
objects and shapes. Concrete folds, paper fruit model, rotated examples, independent
identification/construction/shading/explanation, unequal non-examples and
