P1-18-FEATURES / model
A side here is a straight part of the boundary. A corner is where two straight
sides meet. A square corner fits an undamaged square card corner exactly. A
square has four equal straight sides and four square corners; a non-square
rectangle has four square corners and equal opposite sides but unequal
adjacent sides. A circle has a curved boundary and no straight sides or corners.
P1-18-MODEL / model
From left: square, non-square rectangle, circle. Match square to square,
non-square rectangle to rectangle, circle to circle. Both quadrilaterals have four
square corners and equal opposite sides. Only the square has all four sides
equal here. A square is a special rectangle. Examples: square tile face,
non-square book cover, round lid outline; these approximate ideal flat shapes.

Teaching notes and honest records
Adult guidance and answers
Flat shapes / Educational review edition
Use safe smooth-edged cards or large flat faces on a reachable table. An adult
prepares materials; no sharp edges, small choking hazards or pupil cutting.
Model following a straight side, a corner and a square corner before features.
Compare a safe undamaged square card corner at EVERY corner. Trace safe
flat faces only if resources are available. Without them, record picture
interpretation, not practical tracing or handling.
Keep model/name pages, facing pages and prior answers covered in unaided
work. Collect ALL names before saying any name; hide this record before
named pointing. Before naming, cover the same-page matching-name strip
with a plain sheet, keeping pictures visible. Only AFTER spoken-name pointing
reveal labels for matching. Read labels neutrally. Score naming, recognition,
matching, comparison and all-three examples separately. Accept square as
rectangle with a correct special-case explanation; ask for its most-specific
name without marking the mathematics wrong.
Misconceptions: a turn changes a square; colour names a shape; every
four-sided figure is a rectangle; round solid means circle. Help: turn a safe
card, follow all sides and check all corners; compare square and non-square
rectangle; identify a solid object's FLAT face or outline. Record recall first,
supported help second, then the new unsupported recheck. Fresh transfer and
delayed recall use different turns/order. Never invent mastery or practical
outcomes.

Words: flat: lying in one plane; boundary: all the way round a shape; side: a
straight boundary part here; corner: where straight sides meet; square
corner/right angle: fits a square card corner; opposite: across from;
neighbouring/adjacent: next to; equal: the same length here; square: four
equal straight sides and four square corners; rectangle: four square corners
and equal opposite sides; circle: curved boundary with no corners; repeat unit:
the group used again in the same order.
