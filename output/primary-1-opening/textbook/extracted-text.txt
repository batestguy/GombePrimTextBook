FOR THE TEACHER / ONE TO FIVE
Teach, notice and return
Read pupil text aloud. Use several sessions; pace to the children.
Opening chapters / Educational review edition
Prepare. Large clean unbreakable cups, paper and numeral cards from 1 to 5. Put
materials within reach for seated use. Front matter introduces children/teachers once.
Mrs Binta leads the counting chapter. Mr Aliyu leads the next chapter.
Suggested sessions. Story/concrete model; guided counting and numeral meaning;
count/draw and handwriting; recall, ending and fresh checks. Repeat or subdivide
freely. Counting, numeral recognition, reading and formation are separate observations.
Do not require children to read numbers to start counting.
Model and guide. Touch one cup while saying one number, then state the last number
as the total. Invite a child to try. Use the cup/numeral ladder only after this model. Show
one circle for each cup before drawing tasks. Form each numeral on paper before pupils
trace, then ask for independent writing.
Response surfaces. Use the printed workbook references beside the textbook tasks.
Count and draw, Trace and write, Show how you count and A fresh counting try are
workbook pages. The textbook provides models and the same source tasks for an
ordinary exercise book. Textbook Q5/6 are on Write, show and explain. No task requires
a mark in the textbook.
If needed. For skipping/repeating, begin with two cups and move each counted cup
aside. Recheck with a different small set. For failure to state the total, repeat the last
number and ask how many altogether. For handwriting difficulty, accept spoken or
pointed answers while practising formation separately.
Later recall. Another day, ask what Mrs Binta showed. Offer a fresh set of two or five objects
without a model. Ask for the total and shuffled numeral recognition. Record independent / with
help / not yet. Keep child records private.

Answers and fresh checks
Adult IDs preserve source correspondence. Do not read answers before pupils try.
Opening chapters / Educational review edition
P1-01-Q1: 3 cups. One touch and one number word for each; accept oral total before
written 3.
P1-01-Q2: 5 cups in the scattered group; arrangement does not change the count.
P1-01-Q3: Two separate circles, one for each of the two pictured cups. Any clear
placement is valid.
P1-01-Q4: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 in order. Observe independent formation after tracing, not
tracing alone.
P1-01-Q5: 4. Four fingers or a set of four objects; accept pointing/speaking before
writing.
P1-01-Q6: Touch or move each cup exactly once, say one number word per cup and
state three. The source used tops; cups preserve its counting-method answer.
Other exact examples. Teacher model: 3 cups. Ladder: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 cups. Drawing model:
2 cups and 2 circles. Ending: 4 friends, 4 cups, one cup per friend. Enact the ending with
real cups before the closing dialogue.
Fresh tasks. The adult chooses each requested number from 1-5 and records it privately.
Accept exactly that many objects/circles in any clear arrangement.
Recognition: with cards 3, 1, 5, 2, 4, name a value and ask the child to point.
Reading: point to a card in mixed order and ask the child to name its number. Accept speech or
the child's usual communication method; do not give the value first. Use ordinary paper cards if
needed. Record recognition, reading and formation separately.

FOR THE TEACHER / SORT, MATCH AND ORDER
Teach, notice and return
Mr Aliyu leads. Keep the three-leaf/five-top story inventory consistent.
Opening chapters / Educational review edition
Prepare. Three clean large leaves and five large tops, a real tray/spare box, paper and
safe larger counters. Use pieces too large to swallow and supervise. These are pretend
game tickets and counters. Give seated children reachable materials. Do not ask pupils
to move printed pictures.
Suggested sessions. Story and physical sorting; matching/equal sets and ordering;
independent comparison; recall, support, ending and fresh checks. Change the pacing
to the class. Sort by one rule before comparing quantities. Let every child place pieces
and explain a rule.
Model and guide. Pair two tops to four: two unmatched lower tops show more. The
completed oval demonstrates circling a whole set in the workbook. Equal rows have
three each despite different spacing; any bijection is valid. The completed circle around
the leaf in the second position shows circling one object.
Help and recheck. Restate the type rule; sort one and let the child place another. Pair
objects physically when spacing misleads. After the 2-versus-3-dot support model,
recheck with 1 and 2 objects: 2 has more, 1 fewer. Build small quantity sets before
asking for numeral order.
Ending and fresh checks. Use the original 3 leaves and 5 tops. Make 3 pairs and put the
2 unmatched tops in the real spare box; then read the closing dialogue. Fresh printed tasks
are the same tasks on either response surface, not two independent tests. Also ask pupils to
sort a new physical mixed tray and count its groups.
Delayed recall. Another day give 4 leaves and 2 tops to sort/compare, then order 5, 2, 3.
Answers: leaves/tops; 4 more, 2 fewer; 2, 3, 5. Record independent / with help / not yet.

Answers and story checks
Read as a beginner; check the response surface and allow explanation.
Opening chapters / Educational review edition
P1-02-Q1: 1, 3, 4.
P1-02-Q2: One dot in the left set; three in the right set. Keep the sets separate.
P1-02-Q3: 5 tops, the right set.
P1-02-Q4: 3 leaves, the right set.
P1-02-Q5: Exactly five objects. Any safe larger objects and clear arrangement are
valid.
P1-02-Q6: Match one to one or count both sets. Accept an accurate explanation,
demonstration or drawing; check each object has only one partner.
Other exact examples. Mixed/sorted story tray: 3 leaves and 5 tops. Guided sort: second
object is a leaf already circled; fourth object is the other leaf; tops first and third. Matching
model: 2 pairs and 2 unmatched tops in the four-set. Equal rows: 3 each, all have partners.
Order model: 2, 3, 4 dots.
Support / ending / fresh. Support dots: 2 upper, 3 lower; 2 pairs, 1 unmatched lower dot.
Ending: 3 pairs and 2 spare tops. Fresh sort: leaves are second and fourth; tops first, third
and fifth. Fresh comparison: 5 leaves more than 4 tops; right set. Do not reveal these
answers before independent attempts.
For the fresh physical tray, the adult chooses quantities within 1-5 for each type, records them privately,
then checks the child sorts by type and counts each group once.

Words, evidence and credits
Opening two chapters only; later Primary One content remains in development.
Opening chapters / Educational review edition
Words. Count: give one number word to each object. Total: how many altogether.
Numeral: a written number symbol. Sort: group by a rule. Set: a collection. Match: pair
one with one. More/fewer: greater/smaller number of objects. Same number: all objects
pair with none unmatched.
Historical objective map. One to five: P1-p01-02 (quantity), -03 (count), -04 (write), -05
(order); source tasks Q1-Q6 and additional recognition/fresh tasks. Sort/match/order:
P1-p01-01 (sorting/classification), -05 (quantity order), with more/fewer/same as taught
comparison. Practical sorting/counting must be observed, not inferred only from circling
printed leaves.
