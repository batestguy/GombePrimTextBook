## Proposed structure for each class book

1. Own cover, title/credits, welcome, contents and learning journey.
2. Short character introductions and one page introducing both teachers. A child
   must be able to start this class book without owning another volume.
3. Class-appropriate starting check and support for prerequisite gaps.
4. Carefully sequenced teaching chapters with stories, models, supported practice,
   independent tasks, recall, help, story resolution and fresh checks.
5. Revision and assessment placed at meaningful checkpoints. Suggested term
   divisions remain editorial unless an applicable scheme is verified.
6. Grade-appropriate offline AI content, subject to the placement decision below.
7. That class's teacher guidance, complete answers, interventions, delayed checks,
   glossary, curriculum evidence, credits and an appropriate next-step preview.

Recommendation: keep guidance and answers inside each book, following the existing
preference for self-contained volumes. Do not silently create a separate teacher
edition. Retain English-only, full-colour A4 write-in pages and deterministic
assessed diagrams. Adapt reading demand and character development across classes.

## Decisions before further chapter production

- Confirm final contents and granularity for each class, including missing topics,
  prerequisites, repetition and the role of revision/assessment.
- Obtain and check applicable curriculum objectives. Existing historical evidence
  is useful, but no current NERDC alignment or complete school-year coverage is
  claimed. Missing source editions and Gombe adoption evidence remain recorded.
- Decide AI allocation: a distinct age-appropriate section in every book, or a
  section in Primary 3 only. If retaining four final pupil pages in each book,
  that is twelve newly planned pages across the series, not four reused unchanged.
- Agree reading load, exercise density, writing space and print-cost constraints
  for each class before estimating page budgets. The seven pupil pages in P1-02
  are a worked example, not a required template for every chapter.
- Replace ambiguous Book 1/Book 2 naming with explicit class titles and agree what
  each next-book preview means, especially at the end of Primary 3.

## Proposed delivery sequence

1. Freeze new chapter drafting while establishing the series structure. Preserve
   all existing drafts and approvals as evidence for their exact reviewed versions.
2. Audit existing content against each class's intended objectives; create three
   contents/progression maps with source IDs and open gaps. Review chapter
   granularity before committing to counts or page estimates.
3. Revise conflicting single-volume instructions, plan, page budgets, handoff and
   packaging rules to match the agreed structure. Preserve the protected source
   GombeTextbook.txt byte for byte.
4. Build a shared rendering system with three class-specific entrypoints and
   separate contents, pagination, answer maps and review reports. Chapter 1 and
   Chapter 2 currently use separate experimental renderers; unify their reusable
   components rather than adding another special script for every chapter.
5. Assemble the Primary 1 front matter plus its first two teaching units as a
   complete opening section. Migrate P1-01 into canonical redesign data, fix its
   outstanding current-standard evidence, and re-review assembled pages. Retain
   P1-02's tasks, story and evidence, subject to source/layout freshness checks.
6. Establish representative Primary 2 and Primary 3 chapters to test the design at
   their reading and mathematics levels before scaling across the series.
7. Complete and independently review each book in manageable batches, then check
   cross-book continuity and whole-book completeness. Apply the strict review
   gate to every chapter in the relevant book; one sample cannot approve a class.
8. Obtain local educational review, pupil trials, physical print tests and printer
   requirements. These remain pending until actually completed. No press-ready or
   publication approval is inferred from automated checks.
