How to use this book
1 Look and Talk
Look at the situation. Talk about what you notice and what you could find out. 
Decorative story pictures do not supply exact counts.
2 Learn Together
Read a short explanation. Use objects and the worked example. Exact diagrams 
have clear quantities; their printed scale is not a measuring tool.
3 Try It
Complete questions 1 to 4. Use the spaces for writing, drawing or working. A 
teacher may read instructions aloud.
4 Play or Make
Work with inexpensive safe materials. Take turns. A partner checks your 
method, not only your answer.
5 Check Yourself
Complete questions 5 and 6. Tell how you know. Mark: I can do this / I need more 
practice. Return to objects when needed.
Read in number order
Read the main column before the sidebar. WORDS gives vocabulary, HINT helps pupils, and 
TEACHER is for adults. Questions use lesson ID plus question number in the answer key.
Care for one another
Use large clean counters, blunt sticks, paper and unbreakable containers. Adults handle 
cutting and supervise water activities. Never taste materials. Respect privacy and allow 
fictional survey data.

Today: Count, read, write and order 1 to 5.
1 Look and Talk
Amina sets out bottle tops for a school game. What can we

Put one top down. Say one. Add another 
and say two. The last number tells how 
many. Trace with a finger, then write: 1 2 
3 4 5.
WORKED EXAMPLE
Touch the three dots in row A. Say 1, 2, 3. 
There are 3 dots.
A
B
WORDS
count; number; how 
many
HINT
Use objects first. Say the 
number sentence or 
explain the comparison. 
Check another way.
TEACHER
Move each object after 
counting it. Accept 
pointing or speaking 
before writing.

1 Look and Talk

Artificial intelligence, or AI, is a way people build computer systems to do

Materials, access and safety

AI chapter and safeguarding
The four AI pages are enrichment, not a claim that the legacy mathematics 
curriculum prescribes AI. They need no screen. The classification game is a

information private. An adult should select and supervise any later optional 
device use. The chapter’s safety approach is informed by UNICEF guidance on AI

Mathematics glossary
add — Put amounts together.
area — The amount of surface covered.
array — Objects arranged in equal rows and columns.
capacity — How much a container can hold.
centimetre — A length unit; 100 centimetres make a metre.
compare — Find how amounts or properties are alike or different.
cube — A solid with six equal square faces.
cuboid — A box-shaped solid with six rectangular faces.
data — Information collected or recorded.
denominator — The bottom fraction number; it counts equal parts in a whole.
difference — The result of subtraction.
divide — Share equally or count equal groups.
equal — Having the same value or amount.
equivalent fractions — Different fraction names for the same amount of the same 
whole.
factor — A number that divides a whole number exactly.
fraction — A number describing equal parts of a whole or collection.
gram — A unit for mass in everyday weight measurement.
half — One of two equal parts.
kilogram — 1,000 grams.
