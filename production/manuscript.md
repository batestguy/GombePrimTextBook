## P1-01 One to five

Goal: Count, read, write and order 1 to 5.

### 1 Look and Talk

Aishatu's pretend tea shop needs a cup for every friend. Mrs Binta helps the club count carefully.

### 2 Learn Together

Touch each cup once. Say one number word as you touch it. The last number tells how many. Match each group to its numeral, then practise forming 1 to 5 in a workbook or exercise book.

Worked example: Touch three cups once each: one, two, three. There are 3 cups. A circle can represent each cup: two cups can be shown by two circles.

Vocabulary: count; number; how many

Teacher: Model one touch and one number word per cup, then the total. Check quantity, numeral recognition and formation separately. Read aloud; allow speaking, pointing or moving objects before writing. Work in the workbook or an exercise book.

## P1-02 Sort, match and order

Goal: Sort collections and compare small sets.

### 1 Look and Talk

Aishatu's toy shop is open. The club needs to tidy leaf tickets and top counters for a new game. Mr Aliyu helps them sort and compare.

### 2 Learn Together

A set is a collection. Sort by one rule: leaves together and tops together. Match one leaf to one top. Unmatched tops show which set has more. Sets with a partner for every object have the same number. Count sets and put them in order from fewest to most.

Worked example: Match 2 tops to 4 tops, one to one. Two tops in the set of 4 have no partner. Four is more than two; two is fewer than four. Sets of 2, 3 and 4 go in that order from fewest to most.

Vocabulary: sort; set; more; fewer

Teacher: Read aloud and model sorting before matching. Put one object beside one partner; spacing and object size do not decide which set has more. Provide reachable materials and allow pointing, speaking or moving objects. Recheck later with fresh sets.

## AI-1

Meet artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence, or AI, is a way people build computer systems to do tasks such as recognising pictures or suggesting words. Some AI systems find patterns in many examples. An ordinary calculator follows fixed calculation rules; a device is not AI just because it uses electricity.

Amina sees an app guess the name of a plant from a photograph. An adult explains that the app may use AI. It can still guess wrongly.

Look and Talk: What would you ask before trusting a picture guess?

1. Circle the example that may use AI: a picture-recognition app / a wooden ruler.

Picture-recognition app. A wooden ruler is a physical measuring tool.

2. Tell one thing AI can help people do.

For example recognise patterns in pictures or suggest words; accept a suitable example.


## AI-2

Learning from examples

Some AI systems are trained using examples selected by people. They look for patterns rather than learning exactly as a child does. If the examples are few or unrepresentative, the system may make poor guesses on new cases.

Musa shows a pretend learner only red circles. When a blue circle appears, the learner has not seen that colour before. We need circles of varied colours and sizes to test the idea of circle.

Learn Together: Shape is different from colour. A blue circle is still a circle.

1. Draw a red circle and a blue circle. What shape are both?

Both are circles, regardless of colour.

2. Why should examples include different colours and sizes?

So the pattern is not based only on an accidental colour or size feature.


## AI-3

Play the example game

No device is needed. Make 8 paper cards: red and blue circles and triangles, with two sizes of each colour-shape combination. An adult helps cut them. Put a shape label on each card.

Training round: show one red circle, one blue circle, one red triangle and one blue triangle. Tell the labels. Hide the labels on the four other cards. Test round: ask a partner to predict each label, then reveal it. Record correct and incorrect guesses. Swap roles. This is a simplified analogy, not a working AI system.

Play or Make: Use shape, not colour, to classify the unseen cards. All materials stay offline.

1. What are the two categories in this game?

Circle and triangle.

2. If 3 of 4 test guesses are correct, how many are incorrect?

1 incorrect; 4 − 3 = 1.


## AI-4

Check, ask and keep private

AI can make confident mistakes. Check a mathematical answer using objects, a calculation or a trusted adult. Ask an adult before using an AI app. Do not share your name, photograph, school, address or private family information. You do not need an account or a device for this chapter.

Zainab hears an app say 3 + 4 = 8. She counts seven tops, checks 3 + 4 = 7 with her teacher, and corrects the answer. Daniel says, “A confident answer still needs checking.”

Check Yourself: Explain a safe next step when a device gives a surprising answer.

1. A system says 5 + 2 = 9. Check and correct it.

5 + 2 = 7; show five objects and two more.

2. An app asks for your photo and school name. What should you do?

Stop and ask a trusted adult; do not provide private information.
